# Tumble — "Kinetic Energy" trailer: production bible

A playful, story-driven 83-second trailer for Tumble. A survival player has a lovely
morning walk, admires a cave, meets a creeper and goes on a very long trip to the
bottom of that cave. Then he comes back for his stuff.

Everything on screen is real:

- **The world** was generated by the vanilla 1.20.1 server (seed `8091867987493326313`)
  and read straight out of its region files. The set is a natural cave entrance at
  x 1500, z 139, with a cherry grove to the south-west and a river to the east.
- **Blocks and models** come from the 1.20.1 client jar: blockstate variants, multipart,
  parent models, element rotations, cullfaces, biome tints and animated textures, drawn
  with vanilla's smooth lighting, face shading and lightmap curve.
- **Entities** use vanilla's own model geometry (PlayerModel, HumanoidModel, CreeperModel),
  box UVs, animation formulas, creeper swell and white flash, and held-item transforms.
- **Every ragdoll is simulated by Tumble's own solver**, unchanged: `physics/*` and
  `RagdollSkeleton` are compiled straight from `src/main/java` and stepped at 20 TPS
  with the mod's default config. The creeper launch is the mod's explosion formula; the
  crater is vanilla's `Explosion.explode()` ray march with real blast resistances; the
  impact damage, its threshold, multiplier and cooldown are the mod's, so the hits that
  hurt in the trailer are the hits that would hurt in game.
- **Sounds** are the game's own (creeper fuse, explosion, hurt, steps, cave ambience,
  UI clicks). **The music is original**, sequenced from Minecraft note block samples.

## 1. Research notes that shaped the edit

**Structure.** Game trailers that convert front-load a visceral hook in the first 3–5
seconds (never a logo), establish the premise, escalate to a climax around 70–80% of the
runtime, then resolve on a title and call to action. 60–90 seconds is the sweet spot;
over 90 needs a reason. ([presskit.gg editing guide][pk], [animost][an], [epikton][ep])

**Cutting.** Hard cuts almost exclusively, landed exactly on music beats. Shot lengths
start at 2–4 s while establishing, shrink to 2 → 1.5 → 1 s as energy builds and to
~0.5 s at peak, then breathe for 3–4 s before the end card. Dissolves read as
indecisive; the only fades are from black at the top and to black before the card.
Title cards: five words or fewer, under three seconds, never narrating what is already
on screen. ([pk])

**Minecraft cinematography.** Replay-Mod style keyframed paths rather than hand-flown
moves; FOV 70–85 for a compressed, cinematic world (above 100 looks like gameplay
fisheye); shallow depth of field only on close-ups; high render distance on landscape
shots; time-of-day chosen per shot. ([orion][or], [replaymod][rm])

**Comedy.** Pattern, pattern, break (rule of three). A pause before the punchline. Sound
*leads* a gag to telegraph it (the fuse hiss before the boom) or *follows* it a beat
later so the image lands first (the "oof" after the bounce). Silence is the strongest
cue: the music cuts out the instant the creeper hisses. Animation's advantage is total
control of timing: freeze frames, speed ramps and smash cuts. ([rule of three][r3],
[sfx timing][sfx])

**Camera shake.** Trauma-based: shake = trauma², trauma decays linearly, displacement
from smooth noise rather than random jitter (Eiserloh, *Juicing Your Cameras With Math*,
GDC 2016). ([gdc][gdc])

**Framing a falling subject.** Lead room in the direction of travel; a follow shot
places the audience with the subject; cut on impacts, never mid-flight. ([lead room][lr])

**Assets.** Per Mojang's usage guidelines, videos of the game are allowed (including
monetised ones), while redistributing or modifying the game's assets is not. The pipeline therefore downloads them from Mojang at build time and the
repository never contains them. Minecraft's music is not used: the score is original.

## 2. Treatment

**Logline.** Steve wanted a nice view. He got a very thorough tour of the cave instead.

**Tone.** Deadpan. The camera treats the morning like a nature documentary and treats
the fall like an action film. Steve never reacts, because Steve has one face.

**Rule of three.** (1) A peaceful walk. (2) "Nothing could possibly go wrong." (3) Boom.
Later: blown up, falls down a cave, dies on the very last bounce. And the button: he
comes back, gets his stuff, looks up at the 70 blocks he would have to climb, and lies
down instead.

**Hook.** Open mid-air, in slow motion, over the cave: freeze frame, record scratch,
*"Yep. That's me."* — then rewind to the morning. The audience knows how this ends;
the comedy is in watching it get there.

## 4. Camera language

- **Lenses (FOV):** wides at 70–80, people at 45–55, close-ups at 30–40, POV at the game's
  default 70 so it reads as gameplay.
- **Movement:** every move is a spline or an eased path, never hand-held, except the
  explosion shake (trauma model above). Follow cams are critically damped springs on a
  look-ahead point, so the body always has lead room in the direction it is falling.
- **Speed ramps:** slow motion is used twice, on the cold open and on the blast, so the
  audience can read the body; impacts are always real time, because physics comedy lives
  in the speed of the hit.
- **Transitions:** hard cuts on the beat; one freeze frame (the hook); one rewind; one
  smash cut to black; white flashes only where something explodes or the rewind lands.
  Nothing else.
- **Placement:** cave cameras are chosen by a solver over the real world's occupancy:
  clear of rock by at least half a block, an unobstructed line of sight to every point the
  body passes through in that shot, and as little falling water in between as possible.

## 5. Sound plan

| Cue | Source |
|-----|--------|
| Theme | Original, 120 BPM, note block harp lead, bass, guitar, bell and pling, with bd/snare/hat |
| Fall | Xylophone (note block) glissandi into each drop |
| Death screen | Didgeridoo and bass "sad trombone"; `random/click` on Respawn |
| Steps | `step/grass`, `step/stone`, placed by the walk simulation every 1/0.6 blocks like the game |
| Fuse, explosion | `random/fuse`, `random/explode1-4` |
| Impacts | `damage/hit1-3` (the player hurt sound), with a synthesized low thud on the big ones |
| Cave | `ambient/cave/cave*`, `liquid/water` for the stream |
| Weather | `ambient/weather/rain*`, `ambient/weather/thunder*` |
| Elytra | `item/elytra/elytra_loop` |
| Zombie and sword | `mob/zombie/*`, `entity/player/attack/strong*`, `entity/player/attack/crit*` |
| Chest and items | `random/chestopen`, `random/chestclosed`, `random/pop` |
| Trailer effects | Synthesized: wind, record scratch, tape rewind, whoosh, thud |

Mix: music sits on top during act 1 and the montage. SFX lead in the incident and the
fall, where the music drops out entirely. The loudest moment of the trailer is the
explosion; the quietest is the second after the last bounce.

## 6. How it is built

```
trailer/
  world/      Anvil reader, block model baker, mesher, crater, set dressing, sims
  physics/    TrailerSim.java: Tumble's solver driven against the real world
  render/     three.js renderer (headless Chromium on Mesa llvmpipe), shots, overlays
  audio/      sequencer and mix
  tools/      one-shot pipeline: fetch assets → generate world → build → simulate → render → encode
```

See `README.md` in this folder for how to reproduce the render.
